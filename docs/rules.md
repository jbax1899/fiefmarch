# Working rules

_Version 0.1 — draft for discussion, not yet a complete rulebook._

This is where we're collecting the rules and trial values for Fiefmarch. Some details are settled as a design direction; others are starting points we'll test and adjust. Anything left open here still needs a decision.

## Board and setup

Fiefmarch is intended for 2–4 players. We'll balance the first version around 1v1, then work toward 2v2 and four-player free-for-all. How to keep a three-player game fair is still open.

The board is a square grid, generated from a seed. Terrain and resources are mirrored across the center line for 1v1 and 2v2, or across four quadrants for a four-player free-for-all. The terrain types are plains, forests, mountains, sea, and desert. We may also add hills.

Players secretly place a keep and their starting army within designated regions, then reveal their positions at the same time. We haven't settled the board size, starting armies, or placement restrictions.

## Resources and buildings

There are four resources: **grain, wood, stone, and gold**. They can be represented by cards in a tabletop game. Grain comes from farmland or plains, wood from forests, stone from sites near mountains, and gold from deposits.

Each keep produces a small amount of resources every round. Players can increase their income by building farms, lumber camps, quarries, and gold mines on suitable sites. Resources are spent on recruiting units and constructing improvements. Production rates and costs are still undecided. Tactical ability cards are an idea for later, not part of these initial rules.

If an enemy occupies a resource-producing building, it stops producing immediately. After holding it for a set period, the attacker may be able to pay to take ownership or destroy it. We still need to decide how long that takes and what conditions apply.

Units don't have routine upkeep costs.

## Commands and movement

**Proposed turn structure:** Each player has three commands per round, with players taking one command at a time in alternating order. A unit can activate only once per round. We still need to decide which actions count as commands and how this sequence works with more players.

The starting movement allowances are **2 for foot units**, **4 for cavalry**, and **2 for battering rams**. Entering an open square costs 1 movement point. Forests may cost 2; that's a trial value. Mountains and sea are impassable for now. Diagonal movement and the costs of other terrain types are still open.

## Units and combat

The starting roster is infantry, spearmen, cavalry, archers, and battering rams. All have **3 HP**.

Combat won't use dice. Most units should be fairly close in basic strength, with small bonuses in particular matchups. One proposed example is **+1 for spearmen against mounted units**.

We still need to work out the damage calculation, retaliation, support bonuses, retreats, and how archers handle range and line of sight.

A unit can use its activation to **rest**, recovering 1 HP up to the maximum of 3, as long as no enemy is adjacent. We haven't defined whether adjacency includes diagonals.

## Forts and siege

Players can build forts to protect units. A unit inside a fort gets a defensive bonus, especially against archers. **A captured fort is destroyed** rather than taken over.

Battering rams are for breaking fortified positions. They have 3 HP, move 2, and can't attack units outside forts. They also get a defensive bonus against archers.

The proposed ram attack deals **2 damage directly to a fort's garrison**, ignoring the usual fort protection. There's no separate HP value for the fort: the ram damages the defending unit, and another unit must capture the position to destroy the structure. We haven't decided whether a ram should need a friendly unit beside it to attack.

## Questions before the first full match

- How does a player win: capturing a keep, holding objectives, earning points, or some combination?
- What can a command do? How exactly do attacks resolve, and can defenders retaliate?
- What size is the board, where can keeps be placed, and what does each player start with?
- How much does each building produce, what does everything cost, and when does an occupied building change hands?
- How do archer range, diagonal movement, desert terrain, and fort bonuses work?

We'll need answers to these questions before a complete 1v1 playtest.
