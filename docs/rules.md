# Rules

## Board and setup

Fiefmarch uses a square grid with plains, forests, mountains, deserts, and sea. Hills are another possible terrain type.

Maps are generated from a seed. Terrain and resources are mirrored across the middle for 1v1 and 2v2, or in four quadrants for a four-player free-for-all.

Players secretly place a keep and their starting army inside their assigned area. Everyone reveals their placement at once.

The first version focuses on 1v1. The plan also includes 2v2 and four-player free-for-all.

## Resources and buildings

There are four resources: **grain, wood, stone, and gold**. On the tabletop, players keep them as cards.

Grain comes from farms or plains, wood from forests, stone from sites near mountains, and gold from deposits. A keep provides a small amount of resources each round. Players can build farms, lumber camps, quarries, and mines on suitable squares for more income.

Resources pay for units and buildings. Units have no regular upkeep cost.

An enemy standing on a resource building stops its production immediately. One option is to let the attacker claim or destroy the building after holding it for a few rounds, possibly at a cost.

Tactical ability cards are an idea for later.

## Turns and movement

The starting idea is **three commands per player each round**, taking turns issuing one command at a time. A unit can act only once per round.

Movement allowances:

- **Infantry, spearmen, and archers:** 2
- **Cavalry:** 4
- **Battering rams:** 2

An open square costs 1 movement point to enter. Forests cost 2 as an initial value. Mountains and sea cannot be crossed.

## Units and combat

The starting units are infantry, spearmen, cavalry, archers, and battering rams. All have **3 HP**.

Combat is deterministic, without dice. Units have similar base strength, with small advantages in certain matchups. For example, spearmen get **+1 against cavalry**.

A unit can use its activation to **rest** and regain 1 HP, up to 3 HP, as long as no enemy is next to it.

## Forts and siege

Forts protect the unit occupying them, especially against archers. A fort is destroyed when an enemy captures it.

Battering rams attack fortified positions. They have 3 HP, move 2, and get extra protection against archers. They can't attack units outside forts.

A ram's proposed attack deals **2 damage to the unit defending a fort**, ignoring the fort's protection. Forts have no separate HP. The ram weakens the defender; another unit has to take the square to destroy the fort.

## To decide

- **Winning:** Keep capture, objectives, points, or a combination.
- **Combat and commands:** Damage calculation, retaliation, support, retreats, and which actions use commands.
- **Setup:** Board size, starting armies, keep placement rules, and three-player balance.
- **Economy:** Building costs and yields, plus how occupation and capture work.
- **Other details:** Archer range and line of sight, diagonal movement, desert terrain, fort bonuses, ram escorts, and turn order in larger games.
