# Working rules

*Version 0.1 — design draft, not yet a complete playable rulebook.*

These are the rules we've discussed so far. Some are firm design choices; others are **trial values** for our first playtests. Where a rule is missing, it's left open rather than filled in with an assumption.

## Setup and board

Fiefmarch is intended for 2–4 players. Start with 1v1 for balancing; 2v2 and four-player free-for-all are planned, while three-player fairness is unresolved.

The board is a seeded, procedurally generated square grid. For 1v1 and 2v2, terrain is mirrored across the midline; four-player free-for-all uses mirrored quadrants. Terrain includes plains, forests, mountains, sea, and desert. Hills are under consideration.

Each player secretly places a starting keep and army within a designated starting region. Everyone reveals their placement simultaneously. Board dimensions, starting forces, and placement restrictions are still open.

## Resources and buildings

The four resources are **grain**, **wood**, **stone**, and **gold**, represented by cards in tabletop play. Grain comes from farmland or plains, wood from forests, stone from mountain-associated sites, and gold from deposits.

A keep produces a small, dependable baseline of resources each round. Farms, lumber camps, quarries, and gold mines can be built on suitable sites to increase production. Resources pay for units and improvements; exact yields and costs aren't set. Tactical ability cards are a possibility, not part of the initial rules.

An enemy occupying an economic improvement stops its production immediately. Holding it long enough may allow the attacker to pay to take ownership or destroy it. The required time, cost, and other capture conditions need testing.

There is no routine troop upkeep.

## Commands and movement

**Trial turn structure:** Each player gets three commands per round, with players alternating one command at a time. A unit can activate at most once per round. We still need to decide which activities consume commands and how this works in larger matches.

Initial movement allowances are **2 for foot units**, **4 for cavalry**, and **2 for a battering ram**. Entering open terrain costs 1 movement point; forest costing 2 is a trial rule. Mountains and sea are impassable under the initial rules. Diagonal movement and other terrain costs aren't decided.

## Units, fighting, and rest

The proposed starting units are infantry, spearmen, cavalry, archers, and battering rams. Each has **3 HP**.

Combat is deterministic, with relatively similar baseline strength and small situational advantages. **Spearmen gaining +1 against mounted units** is a trial matchup. The damage formula, retaliation, support bonuses, retreat, archer range, and line of sight all remain open.

A unit may spend its activation to **rest**, healing 1 HP up to a maximum of 3, provided no enemy is adjacent. The exact definition of adjacency still needs to be set.

## Forts and siege

Forts are built defenses. A unit holding a fort gets a defensive bonus, particularly against ranged attacks. **Capturing a fort destroys it**; the attacker doesn't get to reuse the structure.

The battering ram is specialized for attacking forts, not ordinary field combat. It has 3 HP, moves 2, cannot attack an unfortified unit, and has a defensive advantage against archers. The proposed ram attack deals a flat **2 damage to a fort's garrison**, bypassing the fort's usual protection. The ram attacks the defending unit, not a separate fort HP pool; another unit must take the position to destroy the fort. Whether a ram needs adjacent friendly support remains open.

## Decisions needed before a full match

- **Victory:** Capture a keep, hold objectives, score points, or combine those goals?
- **Turns and combat:** What counts as a command? How do attacks resolve, and can defenders retaliate?
- **Map and setup:** How big is the board, where can keeps go, and what does each player start with?
- **Economy:** How much does each site produce, what do units and buildings cost, and how does occupation end?
- **Ranges and terrain:** How do archers, diagonals, deserts, and fort bonuses work?

These questions should be settled through a small 1v1 rules prototype before we expand to other modes.
