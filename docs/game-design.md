# Game design

Fiefmarch is a short, turn-based medieval strategy game about building a domain, raising troops, and fighting for territory.

Players get three actions per round to move, recruit, build, or issue Edicts. Expanding the economy means balancing construction with defense.

## Matches

1v1 games use a 12×12 board and aim for 10–15 minutes. 2v2 and four-player free-for-all use 16×16 boards and aim for about 20 minutes.

Each player starts with a keep, worker, infantry unit, and resources. Starting positions are fixed. The last player or team with a surviving keep wins.

Seeded maps use exact rotational symmetry, with basic resources near each keep and contested sites toward the center. Handcrafted maps follow the same rules.

## Building and fighting

Camps produce Food, Wood, Stone, and Gold. Gold funds Edicts and new keeps. Troops protect camps, stop enemy production by occupying them, and can pilfer them for resources rather than taking them over.

Keeps recruit troops and increase unit capacity. New soldiers can defend immediately but cannot move or attack until the next round. Combat is deterministic, with short movement ranges and simple unit matchups.

Fiefmarch should work in desktop and mobile browsers and as a physical board game. It has a medieval tabletop-diorama look, mostly serious with some humor in House names and small details.
